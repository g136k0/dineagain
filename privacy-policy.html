Website enquiries: data and purposes

The consultation form collects your first name, restaurant name, website, work email, telephone number, email-list size range, monthly guest range, quiet-day preference, requested meeting time and timezone. Its hidden anti-spam field helps reject automated submissions. The browser holds entered details in page memory while the form is open; the code does not save them in cookies or browser storage.

Submitting the form sends the details to our team through the Telegram Bot API. The application has no enquiry database, but Telegram receives and may retain the message. Hosting infrastructure may process IP addresses, request metadata and security logs. A consultation request is not an email-marketing subscription.

We use enquiry information to respond and arrange a meeting. The GDPR basis is steps at your request before entering a contract where you are the prospective contracting party; for restaurant representatives it is our legitimate interest in responding to business enquiries. Necessary business administration relies on contract, legitimate interests or applicable legal obligations, according to its purpose. Security and abuse prevention rely on legitimate interests, subject to balancing your rights. We do not use the consultation form to enrol you in promotions.

The fields are required by the current form to arrange and discuss the consultation. If you do not provide them, this form cannot send the request. Please do not include guest lists, sensitive personal information or passwords.

Restaurant QR signups and email campaigns

This website advertises QR signup tools, welcome emails, promotional campaigns and reporting; it does not itself contain a diner signup form or run those campaigns. When a restaurant activates those services, its signup notice must name the restaurant, explain the specific purposes, identify the providers and link to its own privacy information.

Depending on the agreed setup, subscriber data may include an email address, optional name and preferences, restaurant/source identifier, signup date, consent wording/version and evidence, unsubscribe status and delivery records. Email-open, click or device data may be processed only if the actual service is configured for it and the required notices and permissions are in place. These features are not confirmed as active.

For a QR newsletter signup, the restaurant must obtain a freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous opt-in, keep evidence and provide a working unsubscribe link in every marketing email. Scanning a QR code, dining at a restaurant or requesting a consultation is not itself consent. Any existing-customer exception under Dutch electronic-marketing rules must be separately assessed; it must not be assumed for new QR subscribers. Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it, and marketing objections must be honoured.
